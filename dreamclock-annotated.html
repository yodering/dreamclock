Weight Space Clock

A clock written as if/else, compiled into a neural net with no training, then run once per pixel in a shader. Move your pointer to drag its weights away from the hand-written ones.

Every pixel runs this clock as a neural network.

The clock is ordinary logic. Find the digit under the pixel, look up which segments that digit lights, and check whether the pixel sits inside one. handwired turns every comparison and branch into ReLUs and weighted sums. What comes out is 152 neurons, 10 layers deep, with every weight written by hand. Nothing was trained, and at the center the output is exact.

What I wrote
if (x >= 11.5) slot = 1
What the GPU runs
slot = relu(1 − relu(23 − 2·x))

A fragment shader evaluates that network once per pixel, every frame. The weights live in a uniform buffer, so the page can move them while it draws. Your pointer picks a point on a flat slice through weight space, w₀ + a·u + b·v, where w₀ is the hand-written weights and u and v are random directions.

Near the center the comparisons stay sharp and you can read the time. Farther out, the staircases that pick a digit soften into gradients, segments leak into each other, and the network keeps drawing something that isn't a clock anymore. The scrubber shows any single neuron as its own image, so you can watch one comparison or one segment test by itself.

The last seconds digit is fed in as a fraction for a quarter second before each tick, so you can see a hand-wired network blend from one glyph to the next.

Built with handwired by Cheng Lou (MIT). Pointer or touch: move through weight space. Click: new directions. [ and ]: step through neurons.
